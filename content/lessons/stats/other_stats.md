This lesson is a catch-all for stats questions that don't map cleanly
onto one of the other topics -- things like study design, data
visualization choices, or terminology that spans several areas.

## A few cross-cutting habits worth building

- **Look at your data before summarizing it.** A quick histogram or
  scatterplot catches outliers and skew that a single number (mean,
  correlation) can hide.
- **Ask what the unit of analysis is.** Are you measuring per-person,
  per-transaction, per-day? Mixing units is a common source of
  confusing results.
- **Separate description from inference.** Descriptive statistics
  summarize the data you have; inferential statistics (hypothesis
  tests, confidence intervals) generalize beyond it. Know which one
  you're doing.

If your question is more specific -- distributions, hypothesis
testing, correlation, sampling -- the dedicated lesson for that topic
will go deeper than this overview does.

**Check yourself:** for a question you're currently stuck on, is it
asking you to *describe* data you already have, or to *generalize*
beyond it?
