Correlation and regression both describe *relationships* between two
variables, but they answer different questions.

## Correlation

Measures the strength and direction of a **linear** relationship
between two variables, summarized as a single number (r) between -1
and 1:
- r near +1: strong positive relationship (both go up together)
- r near -1: strong negative relationship (one up, other down)
- r near 0: little to no linear relationship

Correlation says nothing about *why* -- which brings up the most
important warning in this lesson.

## Correlation is not causation

Ice cream sales and drowning deaths are correlated -- both rise in
summer. Ice cream doesn't cause drowning; a third variable (hot
weather) drives both. Always ask what else could explain a correlation
before assuming cause and effect.

## Regression

Goes a step further: it fits a line (or curve) that predicts one
variable from another. Simple linear regression gives you an equation
like `y = mx + b`, letting you estimate y for a new x value, not just
describe the relationship's strength.

**Check yourself:** if two variables have r = 0.9, can you conclude
one causes the other?
